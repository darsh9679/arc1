# D’LIFE image and layout reference

Inspected live on 4 October 2026. Screenshots cover laptop (1366 × 768), phone (390 × 844), and a shorter phone (360 × 640). These are responsive browser viewport checks, rather than tests on physical phones.

## What makes the images work

- **A composed scene leads the page.** The desktop hero gives the room most of the screen. Lighting, furniture, plants, and uncluttered surfaces make the scene easy to read. Short white copy sits over darker areas. [Home](https://dlifeinteriors.com/)
- **Mobile has its own composition.** The homepage loads a separate portrait banner. The observed desktop image was 1280 × 624; the mobile banner was 375 × 700. This preserves the person, cooking area, and headline instead of squeezing a landscape scene into a narrow screen. This asset difference was verified through the rendered image elements. [Home](https://dlifeinteriors.com/)
- **Images explain categories immediately.** The service mosaic names Kitchen, Bedroom, Living, and Dining over recognizable room scenes. On mobile, Kitchen and Dining are wide tiles, with Bedroom and Living paired between them. [Home](https://dlifeinteriors.com/)
- **Repeated framing creates order.** Kitchen examples use consistent tile dimensions and short names. The desktop kitchen section pairs a text column with two images; its mobile models appear in a three-column thumbnail grid. [Kitchen](https://dlifeinteriors.com/custom-made/products/kitchen/)
- **The palette comes primarily from the rooms.** Wood, cream, stone, and muted upholstery give the images coherence; purple is used for branded controls. This is my visual assessment, rather than a claim about their photography process. [Gallery](https://dlifeinteriors.com/gallery/)

## What would suit Trayaksh

Use a small selection of strong real project images, with deliberate landscape and portrait crops. Keep hero copy short and place it where it leaves the room’s focal point visible. Match image framing across project cards. Keep concise room labels and compact image groups on phones.

The reference also has weaknesses: the mobile kitchen introduction remains long, floating estimate/chat controls overlap content, and the gallery stacks large images vertically on phones. Trayaksh’s compact project browser and controlled gallery are still useful for limiting scrolling. The reference should guide image composition and hierarchy, rather than determine every layout choice.
